---
title: Recipe - Pan-Fried Zucchini
---

Hopefully this recipe will be one of my major non-technical contributions to the world. 

It's minimalist and simple, but effective at bringing out the freshness in zucchini.
Cutting the pieces thickly should allow you to hopefully end up with pieces that are crunchy and slightly caramelized on the outside, and yet tender on the inside, thanks to the [Maillard reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction).

Make sure to find the level of cooking that you enjoy most!
Here goes:

**Ingredients**

- Zucchini, cut into *1-inch-wide rounds*
    - This is thicker than you might expect, but this is where the magic lies! The purpose of this is to allow the outside to brown while the inside *steams*.
- Olive oil, or any cooking oil of your choice
- Salt, pepper, and optionally, chilli flakes, all to taste
    - The zucchini will spend a fair amount of time in the pan, so any other spices tend to burn
- A stainless steel or cast iron pan

**Recipe**

1. Drizzle a thin layer of oil into the pan and allow it to get upto temperature on a medium heat
    - N.B. Medium heat is *just below* medium high
2. Season the zucchini rounds with salt and pepper
    - Doing this before putting them in the pan draws water out from the surface, which can give rise to better browning
3. Put them into the pan to fry, and **wait**!
    - Ideally, leave them lying stationary
4. Flip the pieces once there is satisfactory browning on the bottom, and the remaining visible flesh is mostly translucent
    - I like to use a fork and spoon to check and flip them
5. Once the other side is also nicely browned, and the flesh feels soft, remove them from the pan
    - In fortunate cases, browning appears as an even coating across the entire round — it can be splotchy sometimes, but no less tasty!
6. Serve hot and garnish with herbs or shaved parmesan

*Bon appetit!*
